Most guides quote a single number for "WhatsApp chatbot pricing", as if every business paid the same bill. That number is misleading: an inbound FAQ bot may only incur its platform subscription, while a retailer sending promotional broadcasts to Brazil can spend $600 or more using the same underlying technology.

The real driver of your cost isn't the chatbot itself. It's how you use WhatsApp's messaging window.

This guide breaks down Meta's per-message fees, platform pricing, and real-world examples so you can budget with confidence in 2026.

## How much does a WhatsApp chatbot cost in 2026

Let's break down what you'll really pay for a WhatsApp chatbot in 2026.

The answer depends entirely on **how you use it**. Here's a quick overview of the three main scenarios and what they typically cost:

- **Inbound-only (support, FAQs, lead capture):** Your bot replies to users within a 24-hour window. Meta charges **$0** for these conversations. You only pay your chatbot platform subscription, which ranges from **$0 (free tier) to about $90/month** for moderate volume. Total monthly cost: **$0–$90**.
- **Hybrid (inbound + marketing broadcasts):** You send proactive template messages (promotions, updates) outside the free window. You pay both the platform fee **plus Meta's per-message fees**, which vary by region (e.g., ~$0.013 per marketing message in India, ~$0.063 in Brazil). Total monthly cost: **$30–$600+** depending on volume and region.
- **High-volume OTPs and authentication:** Similar to marketing, but utility/authentication templates have lower Meta fees. Still, at scale, Meta fees can add up to **hundreds of dollars per month**.

For Typebot Cloud specifically, production WhatsApp deployment requires the **Pro plan at $89/month**. The Free and Starter plans let you build and test flows in the browser, but they do not include WhatsApp deployment.

The final bill is made up of three main parts: **Meta messaging fees**, your **chatbot platform subscription**, and the **build/setup effort**.

### The three layers of WhatsApp chatbot pricing

1. **Meta messaging fees:** These kick in when you send template messages (marketing, authentication, utility) outside the free 24-hour service window. Meta's rates depend on the recipient's country and the message category.
2. **Chatbot platform subscription (and any API / messaging provider costs):** SaaS for the software that powers your bot (flow builder, inbox, automations). If you use a [Twilio-style API provider](./whatsapp-business-api-providers.mdx) for connectivity, that bill is separate from a marketing platform subscription — same Meta rates underneath, very different product fees on top.
3. **Build/setup effort:** The upfront work can be free if you use a visual no-code builder and go DIY. But hiring an agency (or a developer) for custom integrations can cost thousands.

It's common for vendors to spotlight just their subscription price and leave out ongoing Meta message fees or maintenance labor. **To get a realistic budget, make sure you map your needs:**

- **Inbound-only bots for support or lead collection:** Very low cost — just your platform fee, and Meta fees are usually **$0**.
- **Hybrid or outbound/marketing bots:** You'll pay for both the platform and Meta template message fees. Make sure you check rates for your region and message volume.

## Meta WhatsApp messaging fees and per-message pricing

<Image src="/blog-assets/whatsapp-chatbot-cost/Free-vs-billable-WhatsApp-messages-1783946092986.png" alt="Free vs billable WhatsApp messages" width={1677} height={754} caption="Free vs billable WhatsApp messages" />


Meta switched to a **per-message pricing model** on July 1, 2025.

You’re only charged **when your business sends a delivered template message**, which includes marketing, utility messages sent outside the service window, and authentication messages.

All **free-form service replies sent within the 24-hour customer service window** are free.

### Key cost rules

- **Meta rates update every quarter.** Always check the latest rate card for your recipient’s region before making cost calculations.
- **Free entry points:** If a conversation starts from a Click-to-WhatsApp ad or a Page CTA, the free window extends to 72 hours.
- **Fees by recipient:** Costs depend on your recipient’s country and the category of message you send, not where you’re sending from.
- **Platform markups:** WhatsApp marketing platforms and some resellers add fees or credit systems on top of Meta's base rates. Pure API/messaging providers usually pass Meta pricing through with their own connectivity billing. Typebot does **not** add any markup on Meta fees.

## Chatbot platform and BSP costs compared

<Image src="/blog-assets/whatsapp-chatbot-cost/different-ways-of-sending-whatsapp-business-messages.avif" alt="different ways of sending whatsapp business messages" width={1200} height={543}  />


Beyond Meta's message fees, you also need a platform to build and run your chatbot — and that's where costs can vary significantly.

To use the WhatsApp Business API, businesses connect through Meta's ecosystem (Cloud API or a provider). A **Business Solution Provider (BSP)** is Meta's term for a partner authorized to host or resell that API access. In practice, the products in the market fall into different baskets, even if they're all labeled "BSP" or "WhatsApp API platform":

- **Chatbot builders** (e.g., Typebot, ManyChat, Landbot): Let you build conversational flows visually without code. Best for inbound support, lead qualification, and dynamic multi-step chats.
- **WhatsApp marketing platforms** (e.g., AiSensy, Wati): Layer broadcasts, shared team inboxes, template campaigns, and CRM-style contact management on top of API access. Many add markups or credit systems on Meta's rates.
- **API / messaging providers** (e.g., Twilio and similar BSPs): Focus on a reliable, developer-friendly WhatsApp API rather than a marketing app. Few product features, but strong stability, webhooks, SDKs, and often multi-channel (SMS + WhatsApp). Best when you build your own logic or connect a chatbot builder.
- **All-in-one platforms** (e.g., Freshchat): Add WhatsApp as another channel inside a customer service suite with agents, tickets, and AI already bundled.

How much you'll pay for a chatbot platform comes down to a few things: the monthly subscription, limits on chats or contacts, the number of users or seats, and whether the provider adds fees on top of Meta's base rates.

### Monthly spend checklist

To figure out your own monthly costs, walk through these steps:

**By separating platform subscriptions from Meta’s regional fees, you’ll have a clearer handle on your support budget and growth costs.**

## Practical ways to lower costs over time

If your volume starts to grow, there are a handful of ways to keep your costs down:

- **Maximize the 24-hour window:** Set up flows so users’ needs get resolved within WhatsApp’s free response window. That way, you avoid Meta’s template charges.
- **Use free entry points:** Take advantage of Click-to-WhatsApp ads or Page CTAs. These can extend your service window up to 72 hours for free.
- **Template categorization:** Stick with Utility templates whenever possible. **Meta charges noticeably less for these than for Marketing templates.**
- **Avoid platform markups:** Look for tools with transparent pricing, ones that pass Meta fees through without tacking on extra "convenience" charges.
- **Self-hosting:** If you’re handling lots of chats, self-hosting Typebot means you skip SaaS subscriptions. You’ll just pay for your server and Meta messages.
- **Optimize with analytics:** Use drop-off stats to find and fix sticking points in your bot. **Higher completion rates mean you’re not paying for wasted conversations.**
- **Targeted messaging:** Segment your audience carefully so you don’t blast marketing templates everywhere. That keeps Meta fees and possible SaaS overages down
