### Conversational messaging builds trust, qualifies leads faster, and boosts demo attendance

WhatsApp’s main strength in B2B is fast feedback.

Buyers don’t want to wait around. **82% expect an immediate reply to sales or marketing questions.** On WhatsApp, bots can handle [qualification questions](./lead-qualification-questions.mdx) on budget, pain points, and timeline right there in the chat.

And when someone agrees to a demo, an external scheduler or campaign tool can send an approved [automated reminder template](./whatsapp-business-automated-messages.mdx) 30 or 5 minutes before. Here’s where WhatsApp shines for B2B teams:

- Qualifying leads with quick back-and-forth in a mobile chat instead of making people slog through long forms.
- Booking demos on the spot, connecting leads directly with the right sales rep while their interest is fresh.
- Keeping relationships warm with content, think videos, case studies, or webinar invites, all right there in the conversation.

## WhatsApp B2B Marketing Strategies That Convert

### 1. Customer feedback and NPS surveys

<Image src="/blog-assets/how-to-collect-customer-feedback/customer-feedback-survey-questions-2.avif" alt="customer feedback survey questions" width={1200} height={740}  />

Static feedback forms get ignored in B2B — response rates often stay in the single digits. Running the same survey inside WhatsApp changes that: one question at a time, quick taps on score choices, and follow-ups that adapt to the answer, all inside a thread your customers already have open.

Here's how a typical Typebot feedback flow works:

- On WhatsApp, the bot asks a short NPS or CSAT question with the Buttons block for tappable scores. A Rating block can collect a typed score, but it does not render rating buttons there.
- Conditional logic branches the conversation — a low score triggers "What went wrong?", while promoters are routed to a review or referral request.  
- Every answer is logged to Google Sheets or sent to your CRM with an HTTP Request block, and negative feedback can alert your team on Slack in real time.
- For contacts with a recorded WhatsApp opt-in, pass the post-purchase or post-demo survey timing to an external scheduler or template sender. Outside the 24-hour service window, it must initiate the survey with an approved template; Typebot can continue the flow after the contact replies.

Conversational channels like WhatsApp see **45–60% response rates**, and completion rates jump from single digits to well above 50% when a survey is chat-based instead of a static form.

### 2. Lead qualification chatbots

Qualifying leads around the clock is tough, especially if you are relying on web forms and emails. 

Here’s what that looks like:

- The bot asks direct questions about challenges, who is involved in decisions, and when buyers plan to move forward.  
- Every answer scores the lead in real time. If they are qualified, the flow sends them straight to demo scheduling. If not, they are added to a nurture track.  
- Before syncing, add a Set Variable block with the `Transcript` preset to save the entire chat history in a variable. Map that transcript variable, the score, and the lead details into an HTTP Request block or automation platform for your CRM, like HubSpot or Salesforce. When someone is hot, Slack notifies your reps so they can jump in.

**Buttons and branch logic make conversations personal.** That keeps people engaged and helps you spot high-potential leads with less guesswork.

## Build the B2B Flow in Typebot

### Plan the flow: core steps and design principles

A good B2B WhatsApp flow should do one thing well: **qualify leads quickly and get the right prospects in front of sales**.

Most B2B flows for WhatsApp stick to a few core steps:

- Welcoming the user
- Figuring out their needs
- Branching logic to separate good fits from others
- Scheduling a demo, or nudging less-qualified leads into nurture paths
- Syncing the data to your CRM or Google Sheets

<Image src="/blog-assets/typebot-chatbot-builder-v2.avif" alt="Typebot Chatbot Builder V2" width={1200} height={749}  />

Typebot’s interface is built around connecting visual blocks. Each block displays info, asks a question, saves an answer, checks a condition, or triggers outside actions.

Here’s a look at how a typical lead flow might look in the Typebot editor:

### Each step, block by block

1. Welcome  
 Start with a Text block greeting. You can personalize it using variables, like “Hi, \{\{Name\}\}”.

<Image src="/blog-assets/faq-chatbot/create-a-welcome-message-faq.gif" alt="How to create a welcome message" width={800} height={472} loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

2. Company and need qualification  
Use Input blocks to gather details, think company name, main challenge, timeline. **Each response goes into its own variable.**
3. Branching by fit (conditional logic)  
Add a Condition block to check the answers. Strong leads go straight to scheduling, while others get routed to helpful content or a softer follow-up.
4. Book demo or nurture  
Show clear options using Buttons blocks. If they want to book a demo, send them to a link. If not, offer case studies or queue up a polite follow-up message.
5. Push data to CRM or Sheets  
At the end, use the Google Sheets integration, an HTTP Request block, or an automation platform to send the collected data to your CRM or a team Slack channel.
6. AI qualification (optional)  
You can optionally drop in an OpenAI or Anthropic block if you want to assess open-ended responses or get a quick summary of what the lead wrote.

## What to measure

<Image src="/blog-assets/whatsapp-b2b-marketing/Which-metrics-to-measure-in-your-chatbot-1785764417527.avif" alt="Which metrics to measure in your chatbot" width={1360} height={754}  />

### Response time

Check how long it takes from when someone reaches out to when they get the first automated reply, as well as when a live rep steps in.

**The faster you respond, the more likely buyers are to stay interested instead of dropping off.**

### Qualified-lead rate

Watch the percentage of conversations that meet your core qualification criteria.

This helps you see how well your flow is working. It also makes sure your sales team spends time on leads that actually matter.

### Demo show-up rate

Look at how many scheduled meetings actually turn into completed demos.

When you track attendance, you can tell if automated reminders are keeping people engaged until the call.

| Reminder method | Show-up rate |
| --------------- | ------------ |
| WhatsApp (auto) | ~70–80%      |
| Email           | ~10–20%      |
| Phone call      | ~5–10%       |

### Typebot drop-off and completion rates

Pay attention to where prospects leave the conversation.

If a lot of people exit at the same spot, it’s usually a sign there’s a slowdown or confusing message. You can adjust logic or rework your sequence to smooth out these bumps.

### Opt-out rate

Monitor unsubscribes and opt-outs.

When you see these spike, it usually means your messaging isn’t connecting, you’re sending too often, or you never got clear consent in the first place.

### Quick ROI check

WhatsApp automations move leads through the funnel faster than most outreach, and the ROI is easy to estimate:

```
ROI = (Revenue generated − Total cost) ÷ Total cost × 100
```

- **Costs:** For Typebot Cloud, use Pro at $89/month as the hosted baseline because the Starter plan does not include WhatsApp deployment. Self-hosted deployments replace that plan fee with their own infrastructure and maintenance costs. Before estimating WhatsApp fees, exclude utility templates delivered inside the 24-hour customer service window and every template delivered inside the 72-hour free entry window opened by a Click-to-WhatsApp ad or Page call-to-action. Multiply only the remaining marketing, utility, and authentication template volumes by the current rate for each recipient country. Non-template replies inside the 24-hour service window are also free. See the full [WhatsApp Business API pricing guide](./whatsapp-business-api-pricing.mdx).
- **Revenue:** If your average deal is $5,000 and 20% of demos close, each qualified demo is worth about $1,000. Two extra demos a month represent $2,000 in attributed revenue; compare that with your calculated monthly Typebot, template-message, and tooling costs in the ROI formula above.

**5-minute check:**

1. Count the conversations your flow started in Typebot analytics.
2. Multiply by your qualified-lead rate → qualified leads.
3. Multiply by your qualified-to-booked conversion rate → demos booked.
4. Multiply by show-up rate → demos attended.
5. Multiply by close rate × average deal value → attributed revenue.
6. Exclude templates covered by the 24-hour utility or 72-hour free entry windows, estimate spend from the category and recipient country of the remaining delivered templates, then add your Typebot plan and other tooling costs.
7. Subtract total costs, divide by total costs → ROI.

**Benchmarks:** WhatsApp opens ~98% of messages (email ~20–25%), WhatsApp response rates can reach 45–60%, and typical ROI runs 5–6x. If you're far below that, check response time and drop-offs before adding spend, and track revenue with UTM links, promo codes, or CRM tags.
